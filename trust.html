How our bees stay honest

What our AI employees will and won't do, how they handle your customers' information, and who's in charge.

Our bees talk to your customers in your name, so you should know exactly how they behave. This page covers what they say, who checks their work, what they won't do and what happens to the information they handle. It's written for business owners, and for anyone who has talked to one of our bees.

The examples on this page use Harper Roofing, our sample business.

Bees always say they're AI

Every bee says it's an AI assistant in its first line. On the phone, it names the business, gives its own name and says it's AI before anything else:

"Thanks for calling Harper Roofing, this is Bea, the office's AI assistant. How can I help?"

Texts, chats and emails start the same way: the business's name, the bee's name and the words "AI assistant."

When someone asks for a person

If anyone asks whether they're talking to a real person, the bee tells the truth, every time:

"No, I'm an AI assistant for Harper Roofing. I can book you in now, or have someone from the office call you back. Which would you like?"

If they want a person, the bee hands the conversation to the business's team with the full history attached, so nobody has to repeat themselves. On a call, that means a transfer during office hours or a message for a callback. In a text or chat, the bee lets the team know right away.

This is the one rule owners can't switch off. A bee will never claim to be human, make up personal details or dodge the question.

Review replies and social posts go out in the business's name. They're never signed as the owner or anyone on the team.

People in charge

Every bee has people watching over it.

  • A named Beekeeper. Every business gets a real person on our team who sets up its bees, reads their shift logs and fixes anything that's off. Owners can call or email their Beekeeper.
  • The owner's rules. The owner decides what each bee may and may not do: what it can quote, offer and promise, where it can book and when it works. Bees stay inside those rules, and owners can change them any time.
  • The shift log. Everything a bee does is written down with a time, so the owner can see exactly what was said and done.
  • A trial shift first. For about the first week, each bee drafts and the owner approves. No text, email or reply reaches a customer without the owner's OK. Before a bee answers real calls or chats, the owner and the Beekeeper test it with practice conversations. A bee goes on shift only when the owner says it's ready.
  • Hand-offs. When a conversation needs a person, the bee passes it to the business's team with the full history. Bees hand off when someone asks for a person, when someone's upset, when a question falls outside the rules and whenever the bee isn't sure. Bees don't guess.
  • Emergencies. If someone describes a danger to people, like a fire or an injury, the bee tells them to call 911 and alerts the business right away. Business emergencies, like a roof leak, go to the on-call person the owner chooses.

What bees won't do

  • Make up prices or promises. Bees quote only the prices and terms in the owner's playbook. They don't invent discounts, deadlines, warranties or guarantees, and they never promise what an insurance company will pay.
  • Give medical, legal or financial advice. Bees can book the appointment, share the business's own information and pass the question to the right person. They don't diagnose problems, give legal opinions or tell anyone what to do with their money.
  • Take card numbers by text or phone. When it's time to pay, the bee sends a secure payment link from the business's payment system. If someone reads out or texts a card number anyway, the bee doesn't use it and sends the link instead.
  • Ask for things they don't need. Bees don't ask for Social Security numbers, bank logins or passwords.
  • Pressure anyone. No fake deadlines, no guilt trips, no "only two spots left" unless it's true. Follow-ups run on a schedule the owner approves and stop when someone books, says no or asks to stop. Payment reminders stay polite and never threaten.
  • Fake reviews or pay for them. Bees never write reviews, post them under made-up names, or offer discounts, gifts or anything else in exchange for one. June asks customers after a finished job and doesn't pick who to ask based on whether they seem happy. If someone is unhappy, June also tells the owner the same day.

Texting and calling the right way

Nobody likes a text they didn't ask for. Here's how bees handle it.

  • Permission first. Bees only text and call people who have contacted the business, booked with it or agreed to hear from it. Offers and other marketing texts and calls go only to people who've said yes to them. Calls from a bee follow the same permission rules as any automated call. We won't load bought, rented or scraped contact lists into a hive.
  • No cold calls from an AI voice. A bee never makes the first call. Miles phones a lead only when they asked for a call or agreed to calls; everyone else gets a text or an email. US law treats an AI voice like a recorded one, so that consent has to come first.
  • STOP means stop. When someone replies STOP (or UNSUBSCRIBE, CANCEL, END or QUIT), every bee working for that business stops texting them right away. Plain words count too, like "please stop texting me." They get one text confirming it, and nothing after that unless they reach out again. On a call, anyone can say they don't want more calls, and the bees won't call them again.
  • One list, checked every time. Opt-outs go on the business's do-not-contact list, and every bee checks it before reaching out.
  • Quiet hours. Messages a bee starts on its own, like follow-ups, reminders and offers, only go out during daytime hours in the customer's local time. Bees follow the tighter hours some states set, and owners can narrow the window further. If a customer texts at 11 p.m., the bee can still answer, because they asked.
  • The business is always named. Every text, call and email says which business it's from, and the first text in a conversation says how to opt out. Texts come from numbers set up for the business through the registration process US phone carriers require for business texting.
  • Emails too. Every marketing email names the business and has an unsubscribe link, and unsubscribes are honored.

Owners are responsible for having permission to contact their customers, and our terms say so. Bees are set up to make the right way the default.

Call recordings

Audio recording is off unless the business turns it on.

When it's on, the bee says so at the start of every call, right after it says it's AI:

"Thanks for calling Harper Roofing, this is Bea, the office's AI assistant. This call is recorded. How can I help?"

Some states require everyone on a call to agree before it's recorded, so the notice plays on every recorded call, wherever the caller is. If a caller says they'd rather not be recorded, the bee stops recording for the rest of the call.

Recording or not, a bee keeps a written record of each conversation in the shift log, so the owner and the Beekeeper can check its work.

Your data

Bizzy Bee's customers are businesses. When a bee talks to your customers, we're handling their information for you. Here's what that means.

  • It's used to do your work. Your customers' details and conversations are used to answer, book, follow up, bill, report and fix problems for your business. Nothing else.
  • It's not sold. We don't sell it, rent it or use it to market to anyone.
  • It's not used to train AI for anyone else. We don't use it to train AI models for other businesses. Conversations are processed by the AI providers we use to run the bees, and we choose providers whose business terms say they won't train their models on it. That part depends on our vendors' terms. Ask and we'll tell you who they are.
  • Your customer list is yours. You own your customer list and your data, and you can ask for an export whenever you like.
  • Deleted when you ask. Ask and we'll delete it, except what we need to keep to follow the law, like the list of people who asked not to be texted.
  • When you leave, it leaves with you. If you cancel, we'll send you an export, then delete your data on the schedule in our terms.
  • Your customers can ask too. If one of your customers asks us about their information, we'll tell you and help you answer them.

The details are in our privacy policy.

Security basics

No system is perfectly secure, and we won't pretend ours is. Here's what we do.

  • Limited access. Only your Beekeeper and the people on our support team who need it to help you can see your account and your customers' information.
  • Encryption. We use encryption for data on its way between our systems, your apps and our vendors, and for the data we store. Encryption of stored data is provided by our hosting and storage vendors. Depends on our vendors.
  • Phone networks. Regular calls and texts travel over the phone carriers' networks, which we don't control and which aren't encrypted end to end. That's one more reason bees never ask for card numbers.
  • A short vendor list. We use a small number of vendors for hosting, calls and texts, email and AI. Ask and we'll send you the current list.
  • If something goes wrong, we tell you. If we learn that someone got into your data who shouldn't have, we'll let you know quickly, tell you what we know and help with next steps.

Healthcare and sensitive industries

Dental offices, med spas, insurance agencies and property managers handle information that needs extra care. Here's how bees behave there.

  • Just enough to book. Bees ask only for what's needed to book the visit, like name, contact details and the general reason for the visit. They don't ask for medical history.
  • Clinical questions go to your team. A bee won't say whether a symptom is serious or whether a treatment is right for someone. It books the visit or passes the question to your team.
  • Review replies stay general. June never confirms that a reviewer is a patient or mentions anything about their care.
  • No patient photos without permission. Penny only posts patient photos or stories the practice has written permission to use.
  • Coverage questions go to licensed people. At an insurance agency, bees don't say what a policy covers or recommend coverage. They book a time with the agency's licensed team.
  • Every renter gets the same answers. For property managers, bees give every applicant and tenant the same information, and pass application decisions, accommodation requests and disputes to a person.

Dental offices and med spas are on a waitlist for now. Before bees handle patient information, we sign a business associate agreement (BAA) with your practice and have one in place with every vendor that touches the data: phones, texting, the AI model and storage. Until that chain is complete we won't start a healthcare practice, so we'll take your details and call you when it is. You won't find a "HIPAA compliant" badge on this page. What we can tell you is exactly how bees behave, and we'll go through the rest with you before they start.

Reporting a problem

If a bee got something wrong, we want to hear about it.

  • Business owners: call or email your Beekeeper, or write to hello@thinkabstrakt.com.
  • Talked to a bee? You can ask the bee for a person at any time. You can also contact the business directly, or email us at hello@thinkabstrakt.com with the business's name, the number the bee used and roughly when it happened.
  • To stop messages: reply STOP to any text, or tell the bee on a call.
  • Security concerns: email hello@thinkabstrakt.com with "Security" in the subject line.

A person on our team reads every message. If we got it wrong, we'll say so and fix it.
