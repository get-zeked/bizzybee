Where the work slips

You know these days.

The work is never the problem. It's everything that rings, pings and piles up while you're doing it.

The first 100-degree week

Every system that limped through spring gives out at once. Your office has one caller on the line and three more ringing through to whoever's next on Google.

No heat at 11 PM

For a family with a newborn, it's an emergency. For a couple with a space heater, it's a first-thing-tomorrow job, and somebody has to ask before your on-call tech gets woken up.

Maintenance plans that quietly lapse

Members are your steadiest work, but tune-up reminders slip when you're slammed. A plan nobody renews turns into a customer who calls someone else when the compressor goes.

How does Bea decide what's an emergency?

She follows the rules you set with your Beekeeper, like no heat when it's under 40 degrees outside, or no cooling with a baby or an older adult in the house. A gas smell gets one answer: leave the house and call 911 or the gas company. Then she alerts your on-call tech.

Can Bea tell plan members from everyone else?

Yes, once your customer list is connected. She checks whether a caller is on your maintenance plan and offers the priority times and pricing the plan includes.

Will Bea quote repair or replacement prices?

She shares what you've put in the playbook, like your diagnostic fee and after-hours rate. Repair and replacement prices come from your technician once they've seen the system.

What happens when every slot is full during a heat wave?

Bea offers the next open time instead of overbooking your techs. With Ruby on shift, the caller goes on the waitlist and gets a text as soon as a slot opens up.
